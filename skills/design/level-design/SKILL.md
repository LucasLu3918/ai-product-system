# Level Design

Use for spaces, encounter sequences, onboarding, and difficulty pacing. Use game-balance-economy when the main question is numerical balance.

## Method

1. State the level’s player goal, expected knowledge, constraints, and desired emotional rhythm.
2. Sketch critical paths, optional paths, landmarks, sightlines, safe areas, decision points, and failure/recovery routes.
3. Introduce a new mechanic in a safe context, provide a low-risk practice, then test mastery with a fair challenge.
4. Pace tension and recovery; vary pressure without obscuring objectives or relying on unseen rules.
5. Check spatial readability, accessibility, navigation cues, and whether the layout supports intended strategies.
6. Validate with representative playtests. Record where players hesitate, miss affordances, or use unintended shortcuts.

## Output

Provide a beat map or layout, mechanic-teaching sequence, difficulty assumptions, readability risks, and playtest questions. Keep observations separate from designer inference.
